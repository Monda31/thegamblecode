Roulette bets, odds and payouts (European wheel)

By The Gamble Code · Updated 2026-11-13 · How we calculate

Every standard bet has a different payout and a different chance of winning, but the same cost to you.

Short answer. It is 2.70% on every standard bet: straight up, split, street, corner, six line, dozen, column and even-money bets all have the same expected loss.

How to read it

The chance of winning is the number of pockets covered divided by 37. The expected result is that chance times the payout, minus the chance of losing. It comes out at -1/37, or -2.70%, for every row. A higher payout means a lower chance of winning. It does not mean a better deal.

Which bet should you pick?

  • Even-money bets give small, frequent results and smoother sessions.
  • Straight-up bets give long dry spells and the occasional big payout. The average cost is the same.
  • Many betting plans use even-money bets or a few numbers at a time. The plan decides how much you can lose per attack, not how likely you are to win.

Zero and special rules

If zero comes up, standard bets lose. Some tables use "la partage" or "en prison" on even-money bets, which returns half the stake or holds it for the next spin. These rules halve the edge on those bets, to about 1.35%. Check the table rules before you play.

Quick answers

Which roulette bet has the best odds of winning?

Even-money bets (red/black, odd/even, high/low) win most often, with a probability of 48.65% on a European wheel. Their expected result is still -2.70%.

What does a straight-up bet pay?

35 to 1, with a 2.70% chance of winning (1 pocket out of 37).

What is la partage?

A table rule that returns half of an even-money bet when zero comes up, which halves the edge on those bets to about 1.35%.
