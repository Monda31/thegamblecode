Is roulette rigged?

By The Gamble Code · Updated 2026-11-13 · How we calculate

A licensed casino does not need to cheat. The house edge already guarantees its profit over time.

Short answer. A licensed casino does not need to rig roulette: the house edge of about 2.7% on a European wheel already guarantees its profit over time.

Why cheating is unnecessary

A European wheel pays 35 to 1 on a single number that has a 36 to 1 chance of showing. That 1/37 gap, about 2.7%, is built into the game. Across thousands of spins the casino earns that margin without touching the wheel. See the full breakdown in house edge explained.

Can a physical wheel be biased?

In theory a wheel with a defect can favour some numbers. Casinos know this: they inspect and rotate their wheels and record results. Modern wheels are made to tight tolerances, so a usable bias is very rare. Anyone trying to find one would need thousands of recorded spins to tell a real bias from ordinary randomness.

What about online roulette?

  • RNG roulette uses a random number generator. Licensed operators are tested by independent labs for fairness.
  • Live dealer roulette uses a real wheel streamed from a studio.
  • Unlicensed sites are a real risk: nobody can check the game, and withdrawals may be blocked. Stick to licensed operators in your country.

Why it feels rigged

Long streaks happen more often than people expect (see the Monte Carlo fallacy). Add the zero, which makes red/black bets lose slightly more than half the time, and a bad session feels like a trick when it is just variance plus the edge.

Practical advice. Do not look for a bias or a pattern to beat the game. Choose the European wheel, set a budget and a stop-loss, and play for entertainment.
