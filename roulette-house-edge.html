Roulette house edge explained: 2.7% vs 5.26%

The house edge is the one number that tells you what roulette costs you in the long run.

Where the edge comes from

A European wheel has 37 pockets: 1 to 36 plus a single zero. A bet on one number pays 35 to 1, but the true odds are 36 to 1. That one missing unit is the edge: 1/37, or 2.70%. An American wheel adds a double zero (38 pockets), so the edge is 2/38, or 5.26%.

WheelPocketsHouse edgeExpected loss per 100 units staked
European372.70%2.70 units
American385.26%5.26 units

It is the same on every bet

Red/black, odd/even, dozens, columns, a single number: every standard bet carries the same edge. The type of bet changes how bumpy the ride is, not what it costs on average.

An example

If you place 100 spins of 1 unit each on a European wheel, you will on average lose about 2.7 units. On an American wheel, about 5.26 units. In any single session you can be well above or below that. Variance is large, so a few good sessions say nothing about the long run.

What you can control

  • Pick the European wheel. The edge is roughly half.
  • Choose a budget first and treat it as the price of entertainment.
  • Look for "en prison" or "la partage" rules on even-money bets. They halve the edge on those bets when zero comes up.
  • Cap your session with a stop-loss. See how to set one.
