How to set a stop-loss for roulette

A stop-loss does not make you win. It decides, before you play, how much a session is allowed to cost.

Step 1: decide the budget before you sit down

Pick an amount you would be fine spending on entertainment and could lose entirely. Write it down. Do not top it up during the session.

Step 2: set the stop-loss

For most players the stop-loss is simply the budget. If you want to be stricter, stop at 50% of it. The key is that the number exists before the first spin and that you leave when you hit it.

Step 3: size your bets from it

If a method can risk 66 units in one attack, your budget should cover several attacks. Divide the budget by the maximum risk per attack to see how many attacks you can afford. Fewer than 3 means the unit is too big.

Step 4: add a stop-win if you like

Some players also pick a target, for example +20% of the budget, and leave when they reach it. It will not change the expected result but it prevents giving everything back in the same session.

Step 5: log every session

Write down budget, result and time. After ten sessions you will see what roulette actually costs you. Our free session tracker (italiano) is made for this.
